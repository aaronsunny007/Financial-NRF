\title{Reference-Free Reliability Scores Cannot See Corrupted Evidence:\\
A Controlled Test for Numerical Retrieval-Augmented QA in Finance}
\author{Aaron Prakash Gandi\\\small Independent researcher\\\small\texttt{aarongandi007@gmail.com}}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reference-free scores for retrieval-augmented generation judge a response against the evidence that was retrieved, without the gold answer.
Their structural limit is plain: if the retrieved evidence is wrong, a faithful answer is still wrong, and a score computed against that evidence cannot tell.
We test this directly for numerical financial question answering.
We replace a controlled share of the numbers in the retrieved evidence with wrong values and ask which measures notice, using FinQA and TAT-QA and generators from 1.5B to 7B parameters.
Accuracy falls toward zero.
Numeric-overlap support, entailment support, lexical groundedness, a hallucination flag and verbalized confidence do not move, and cannot tell a fully corrupted case from a clean one (AUROC \aucFinoverlap{} on FinQA, \aucTatoverlap{} on TAT-QA for numeric-overlap support, pooled over runs).
Support computed against the \emph{uncorrupted} evidence does (AUROC \aucFinvsclean{} and \aucTatvsclean{}), but needs the reference these scores are meant to replace.
The same measures are near chance as detectors of wrong answers and of real retrieval misses.
Along the way we correct a scoring flaw in an earlier version of this work, show that an evidence-noise manipulation we first used did not degrade the model and therefore tested nothing, and show that composite-score rankings built from five-point correlations or weight sweeps are unstable.
Code, per-response outputs and a pipeline that reproduces every result on free CPU runners are public.
\end{abstract}
