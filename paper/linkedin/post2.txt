Last week I showed that wrong numbers fool AI reliability scores. The obvious reply: "wrong numbers are easy to catch."

So I tried the harder case. I kept every number real and just put some in the wrong place: swapped the years in a table row, or swapped two rows. This is what a badly parsed table does.

Same AI, same questions. On one dataset accuracy fell from 62% to 8%.

Every reliability score I tested that doesn't know the right answer stayed between 0.43 and 0.55. Where 0.5 is a coin flip. I ran it on two model families, Qwen and Llama.

One check did notice: comparing each table cell (row, year, value) against a trusted copy of the table. It caught the swaps 87% to 99% of the time.

So the fix isn't a smarter score. It's keeping a trusted copy of the source and checking against it.

I put the results on a page you can click through: no sign-up, nothing to install.

Do you check the documents your AI reads, or only what it says back?
