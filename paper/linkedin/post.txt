I gave an AI the wrong numbers to read. Its reliability scores barely noticed.

This started as my dissertation project. I've since turned it into an independent research paper.

The setup: an AI answers finance questions by reading company reports. Many tools also give each answer a "reliability score" without knowing the right answer. Mostly they check one thing: does the answer match the text the AI was given?

So I swapped the numbers in that text for wrong ones. Accuracy fell from 53% to 8%. The score that checks whether the answer's numbers appear in the text went from 0.78 to 0.75.

Why: if the text itself is wrong, an answer that matches it still looks fine. The score can't see the problem.

I repeated this on two datasets and three AI models. None of five such scores could reliably tell wrong text from right text. Comparing against the original clean text did work, but that means you already need the right text.

I also found and fixed two mistakes in my own earlier work. They're explained in the paper.

It's a draft preprint, not peer reviewed. Code and results are public.

If you use AI on financial documents, how do you check that the document itself is right?
