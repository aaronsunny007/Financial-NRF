What runs here. The evidence-support score, hallucination detector and gold-free arithmetic verifier execute live in your browser on whatever you paste into the Analyser tab. What does not. The language model. All reported results were produced with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct under 4-bit quantisation on a local NVIDIA RTX 3050; the results tabs show recorded outputs rather than re-running generation.

Paste retrieved evidence and a model response. The framework computes evidence support, flags unsupported numbers, and independently re-executes the response's own arithmetic — without ever seeing a correct answer.

Experiment 2 — Hallucination detector sensitivity (n = 10)

Scoring note. These recorded hallucination rates were scored while the model’s own “Confidence: 0.9” was counted as an unsupported number. Corrected, the rate is 0.00 in all three conditions (confirmed on a full re-run), so the 0.10 → 0.20 rise below was that artefact. See Method & audit.

An initial two-condition design produced eight of ten byte-identical responses between clean and adversarial conditions. A manipulation check revealed why: retrieval had removed every injected sentence before generation. A third condition and an explicit check were added, because without the check a null result cannot be distinguished from a failed manipulation — readings with opposite implications.

  • Retrieval filtered 80% of document-level distractors — only 2 of 10 reached the evidence block.
  • Once contamination reached the model the flag rate doubled and evidence support declined.
  • The generator never adopted an injected value, so movement reflects contaminated context rather than contaminated answers.

These deltas correspond to single examples at this sample size and the intervals overlap heavily. The mechanism is demonstrated; the magnitude is not established. The accuracy rise under evidence injection reflects one example flipping and must not be read as noise improving performance.

Confidence on correct and incorrect answers differs by 0.024 on a unit scale, so the stated signal carries almost no information about correctness, while 64% of responses were confidently wrong. In a financial decision-support setting this is the concrete high-risk failure mode the framework was designed to expose, and it is the most robust single finding in the project.

This is self-reported confidence elicited in the prompt, not a probability derived from token log-probabilities.

Why the composite fails to track noise

Because self-reported confidence is near-constant at ≈0.90, calibration error becomes an almost deterministic function of accuracy:

NoiseAccuracyECE observed0.90 − accuracy
0%0.34000.55600.5600
10%0.30000.58000.6000
20%0.38000.49200.5200
30%0.36000.52400.5400
40%0.32000.56800.5800

The nominal 0.30 weight on accuracy is therefore in practice ≈0.50, with a further 0.20 entering through the degenerate calibration term. The composite inherits accuracy's full instability, which at this sample size exceeds the effect being measured.

Closed-book scores zero. No sampled item is answerable from parametric knowledge alone: the benchmark does not leak, and retrieval accounts for the entire accuracy of the system. The detector flags every closed-book response, a positive control that complements Experiment 2 by moving the flag rate from 0.08 to 1.00 rather than 0.10 to 0.20.

The bottleneck is reasoning, not retrieval. Perfect evidence raises accuracy only to 0.420. Approximately 12% of remaining error is retrieval-attributable; 88% is bounded by the generator. This retrospectively explains why two retrieval modifications, attempted earlier and reverted, could not have succeeded — their entire available headroom was eight accuracy points.

A second, independent failure of the composite. The oracle condition is more accurate than the retrieval condition, yet the composite ranks it lower (0.5811 against 0.5891). Unlike the correlation argument in Experiment 4 this does not depend on sample size or trend analysis: one configuration is unambiguously more accurate on identical items under an identical scorer, and the aggregate orders them the wrong way.

The lower oracle evidence-support figure is an artefact of evidence size, not degraded grounding: gold evidence is a smaller block containing only strictly necessary lines, so fewer response numbers find a literal match.

Could different weights fix the composite?

Scoring note. The sweep uses the recorded component values. On the corrected scenarios, and on the measured float32 re-run (0.1% track noise, 0.0% rank correctly), the share of weightings that pass both tests is still 0.0%. See Method & audit.

The composite was run with one frozen weighting: 0.30 accuracy, 0.30 NESS, 0.20 hallucination and 0.20 calibration. Both of its failures could be blamed on that choice. Why no weighting passes both

The two tests need weightings that don't overlap. Every weighting that tracks noise puts at most 0.35 of its weight on accuracy and calibration combined. Every weighting that ranks correctly puts at least 0.50 there. Only hallucination rate (r = +0.76) and NESS respond to noise, but the oracle condition scores lower than retrieval on exactly those two (NESS 0.628 vs 0.715, hallucination 0.20 vs 0.08), so its advantage shows only in accuracy and calibration. Accuracy barely moves with noise at n = 50.

The failure belongs to the components, not the weights. Re-weighting is necessary but not sufficient. Five noise levels and three conditions of n = 50 are a thin basis, and the sweep selects and evaluates on the same data, so this should be repeated on a larger held-out run.

Methodology audit

Several defects were identified and corrected before any figure was accepted. One more was found afterwards and is listed first, because it changes some recorded numbers.

  • Confidence value counted as unsupported evidence (found after the runs). Evidence support counted the number in the model’s own “Confidence: 0.9” line as one the retrieved text had to support. It never could, so every response was marked down and some were pushed under the hallucination threshold by it alone. The scorer, and this page’s analyser, now ignore the confidence line; the recorded runs were scored the old way and have not been re-run. Measured from the saved files: Exp 1 strict hallucination 0.08 → 0.00; Exp 2 hallucination 0.10 → 0.20 becomes 0.00 → 0.00; Exp 5 ONRS retrieval 0.5891 → 0.6224 and oracle 0.5811 → 0.6211 (ranking still inverted, now a near tie); Exp 4’s noise–hallucination correlation (+0.76) was then measured on a full float32 CPU re-run of all five experiments at −0.12, so it is not supported; the composite’s correlation with noise was +0.62 there. Accuracy fell from 0.34 to 0.20 from numeric precision alone. No weighting passes both tests in any scenario. Calibration and the verifier are unaffected.
  • Fabricated evidence-support constant. A missing dictionary key meant the metric silently returned exactly 0.8 for every example in every condition, pinning 30% of the composite weight to a constant.
  • Two gold-answer scaling defects. Percentage values were mis-scaled by a factor of 100 in two distinct ways, marking correct answers wrong.
  • Chunking defect. A string assigned where a list was expected caused every document to be fragmented into single-character retrieval chunks — verified directly, a 57-character string producing 57 chunks — invalidating two experiments including their baselines.
  • Verifier extraction defect. Expressions following a sentence boundary were mis-parsed because a full stop is both decimal point and terminator. Re-scoring confirmed the pass rate was unchanged, so no reported figure moved.
  • Evidence-support revision. The strict metric penalised correct arithmetic, since a computed answer never appears verbatim in source text. The revision was validated against gaming: an initial implementation showed an 86.5% false-positive rate on invented values and was rejected; the adopted version measures 0.2% over 400 adversarial trials.
  • Two retrieval modifications were implemented, measured and reverted when verification showed they did not fix their target cases — during which ~28% of answers were found to change under prompt perturbations unrelated to the question, establishing the noise floor for all later comparisons.
